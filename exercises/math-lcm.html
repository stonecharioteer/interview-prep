Math: LCM Using GCD

What is LCM?

Definition

The Least Common Multiple (LCM) of two integers is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by both. For example, LCM(4, 6) = 12 because 12 is the smallest number that both 4 and 6 divide into evenly.

Real-World Use Cases

Scheduling: Two traffic lights cycle every 4 and 6 minutes. They coincide every LCM(4,6) = 12 minutes.

Fraction arithmetic: To add 1/4 + 1/6, find the common denominator LCM(4,6) = 12.

Gear ratios: Finding when gears with different tooth counts re-align.

Music: Finding when two rhythmic patterns with different beat lengths coincide.

What This Exercise Teaches

New Concept: Composing Simple Operations

You already know how to compute GCD. The LCM formula shows how a single elegant relationship connects two concepts: 

LCM(a, b) = (a * b) / GCD(a, b)

This is a powerful pattern in algorithm design: build complex operations by combining simpler, already-solved operations.

Complexity

Time: O(log(min(a, b))) — dominated by the GCD computation.

Space: O(1).

Speed

Pseudocode

function lcm(a, b):
    return (a * b) / gcd(a, b)

Or, to avoid integer overflow for large numbers:

function lcm(a, b):
    return (a / gcd(a, b)) * b

Why Does the Formula Work?

The Relationship

Every pair of numbers a and b satisfies: a * b = GCD(a, b) * LCM(a, b).

Think of it in terms of prime factors. GCD captures the shared factors, LCM captures all factors (taking the maximum power of each prime). Together they account for every prime factor from both numbers exactly. 

Example: 12 = 2^2 * 3,  8 = 2^3
GCD = 2^min(2,3) * 3^min(1,0) = 2^2 = 4
LCM = 2^max(2,3) * 3^max(1,0) = 2^3 * 3 = 24
GCD * LCM = 4 * 24 = 96 = 12 * 8

Common Pitfalls

1. Integer overflow

Computing a * b first can overflow for large numbers. Always divide by GCD first: (a / gcd) * b. Since GCD divides a, the division is exact.

2. Zero inputs

LCM(0, n) is typically defined as 0, since there is no positive multiple of 0. Check for zero before dividing.

What You Learned

Key Takeaways

LCM(a, b) = a * b / GCD(a, b) — a clean formula built on a solved subproblem.

Composing simple algorithms to build more complex ones is a fundamental design principle.

Watch out for overflow — divide before you multiply.
