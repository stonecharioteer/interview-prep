Heap: Max-Heap (Insert & Bubble Up)

Why Max-Heap?

Real-World Examples

Job scheduler: A max-heap lets the scheduler always pick the highest-priority job next. Insert jobs with their priority, and extract_max returns the most urgent one in O(log n).

Bandwidth allocation: Give the most bandwidth to the highest-priority stream by maintaining a max-heap of requests.

K largest elements: Use a max-heap to efficiently find the top-K items in a data stream.

Dijkstra variant: Finding the longest path in a DAG uses a max-heap instead of a min-heap.

Max-Heap = Min-Heap with Flipped Comparison

A max-heap is structurally identical to a min-heap. The only difference is the comparison direction: where a min-heap swaps when child < parent, a max-heap swaps when child > parent.

Everything else — the array representation, the parent/child index formulas (parent = (i-1)//2, left = 2i+1, right = 2i+2), and the bubble-up/bubble-down mechanics — stays the same.

Flipping the Conference Hall

In the #60 min-heap tutorial we pictured a conference hall where the shortest people sit at the front. A max-heap simply reverses the seating rule: now the tallest people sit at the front. Each person must be taller than the two people directly behind them. When a tall newcomer sits in the back row, they swap forward until they find someone taller, or reach the front. Everything else — the row-by-row seating, the index arithmetic, the bubble-up path — is exactly the same.

The Approach

Insert into Max-Heap

1. Append the new value at the end of the array (next available position in the complete tree).

2. Bubble up: Compare with parent. If the new value is greater than the parent, swap them. Repeat until the heap property is restored or you reach the root.

Time & Space Complexity

Time: O(log n) — bubble-up traverses at most the height of the tree.

Space: O(1) — insertion is in-place (ignoring the array resize).

Speed

Pseudocode

class MaxHeap:
    def __init__(self):
        self.data = []

    def insert(self, val):
        self.data.append(val)
        self._bubble_up(len(self.data) - 1)

    def _bubble_up(self, i):
        while i > 0:
            parent = (i - 1) // 2
            if self.data[i] > self.data[parent]:   # flipped from min-heap
                swap self.data[i], self.data[parent]
                i = parent
            else:
                break

Common Pitfalls

1. Using the wrong comparison

The only change from min-heap to max-heap is swapping < for > in the comparison. Everything else stays identical. A single wrong operator gives you a min-heap disguised as a max-heap.

2. Confusing extract_max with peek

peek returns the root in O(1). extract_max removes the root, moves the last element up, and bubbles down — that is O(log n).

What You Learned

Key Takeaways

A max-heap keeps the largest element at the root.

It is literally a min-heap with the comparison flipped.

Insert is O(log n) via bubble-up; the root is always the max.

Real-world use: job schedulers, priority queues where highest priority comes first.
